Only aggregate evidence is reproduced here

Historical snapshot, not a live university feed. Private working reports and individual records are not distributed.

Raw paper totals favour larger units. I compared output per academic alongside journal mix.

I built a comparison the department could inspect

I combined publication records, a 31-academic roster and a 24-unit peer dataset. The Excel evidence base and interactive report keep department and peer-benchmark counting rules separate.

The client can compare output, journal mix and years with each definition visible.

How I made the comparison

Follow the evidence / pin a stage

01 / 24 units · one peer dataset

Define the comparison

The comparison covers Malaysian and regional chemical engineering units. Different organisational boundaries limit comparability.

02 / 22 units with staff counts

Ask who is in the denominator

UCSI and NTU lack staff counts. Their paper totals remain in the data, but they are excluded from papers-per-academic plots.

03 / 3.05 papers / academic / year · 78% Q1+Q2

Locate UPM on two measures

Papers per academic adjusts for unit size. Q1 and Q2 are the top two journal ranking bands (quartiles); their share uses only ranked journal articles and reviews. Neither measure captures all research value.

04 / 1,755 credited · 1,769 benchmark papers

Keep the populations apart

The department profile and peer search include different papers. Their totals stay separately labelled.

The comparison compass

Size and journal mix point in different directions

UPM records 3.05 papers per academic per year and a 78% Q1+Q2 share in this benchmark. Select a scope, then tap or focus a dot to inspect a unit.

Changing the window changes the rate

The recent rate is 3.05 versus 2.75 over the longer window. Both use the benchmark staff count; this does not show that individual staff became more productive.

The department's own series supplies context

The department series peaks at 152 papers in 2020, with 100 in 2025 and 65 in partial 2026. Its counting rule differs from the peer benchmark.

Two valid extracts can answer different questions

The profile matches department academics and qualifying addresses; the peer search uses affiliations. Their totals answer different questions.

Comparison rules

A benchmark, not a verdict

Same time window

Peer measures use 2022–2026 together. The source treats 2026 as 0.75 of a year. The October snapshot is not a completed 2026 result.

Known population

The department's credited extract requires a qualifying UPM address, including the department, SPERC, the faculty alone or a UPM research institute under the documented rule.

Label the journal base

Benchmark Q1+Q2 shares use quartiled journal articles and reviews. Chemical engineering CiteScore categories take priority; the nearest earlier available year is used when necessary.

What this misses

  • A current staff count is not historical staff-year exposure.
  • Unit boundaries and database affiliation coverage differ.
  • Journal placement does not measure teaching, translation or societal benefit.
  • Recent work has had less time to accumulate citations; no citation ranking is inferred here.
  • The department and peer extracts use different rules. Two peer units lack staff counts and are excluded from per-academic plots; no performance improvement is claimed.

What I'd do next

Validate each peer's organisational scope and staff snapshot date with the department before using the comparison for planning.
