06 / Client work

A university Solid Waste Management course, rebuilt from scratch

I rewrote a full university course on solid waste: 7 lecture decks with 571 slides, an offline web companion with 11 calculators, and notes for lecturers.

At a glance

Client
A UPM course team, for a university Solid Waste Management course
What I did
  • Rewrote every lecture deck from scratch, Malaysia-first.
  • Built a one-page web companion with calculators, quizzes and a glossary.
  • Covered the calculators with automated tests and fixed every error an independent audit found.
What they got
One shared folder of 51 files with a start-here guide, ready for undergraduate and postgraduate classes.
Tools
PowerPoint, HTML, JavaScript, automated tests
My role / time frame
Freelance research and data work · about two days, 30 Sep to 1 Oct 2026

By Faris Erhan · Portfolio revised 17 October 2026

The course covers solid waste from generation and collection to treatment and landfill. It is used for both undergraduate and postgraduate classes.

The old lecture decks were used as reference only. I wrote every deck again from scratch, with Malaysian data first and global benchmarks for comparison. Key terms are given in English and Malay, and the decks follow the university's colour style.

One set of slides, two audiences

There are four core modules (generation, collection, treatment and landfill) and a fifth capstone module on current issues. Treatment is large, so it is split into three decks: recovery and sorting, thermal treatment, and biological treatment.

71 slides are postgraduate deep dives. Instead of keeping two separate sets, I tagged those slides so the undergraduate copy hides them automatically. That gives an undergraduate and a postgraduate version of each deck, 14 decks in all, from one source.

A web companion students can use

A single offline web page goes with the decks, so lecturers can upload it to the learning platform. It has 11 calculators, about one per module topic, for example combustion and energy, landfill sizing and biogas. Students change the inputs and see the results update.

The calculators' maths is covered by an automated test file, and every test passed. The companion also has 103 self-check quiz questions taken from the decks, and an 86-term glossary.

Checked before handover

Each module also has written lecture notes and a lecturer guide. The course includes five assignments, each with a marking rubric and mapped to the course learning outcomes.

Every assessment pack went through an independent audit. It found between 9 and 26 errors per module, and all were fixed before handover. Quiz answer keys were checked separately, because automated drafts often had the wrong key.

The finished pack was one shared folder of 51 files, with a start-here guide and a list of 100 items for the lecturer to confirm.

What this misses

  • No student results are reported here.
  • The 100 items on the lecturer's checklist still need the lecturer's confirmation.
  • Assessment content is not shown here.

What I'd do next

After the first class, look at which self-check questions students get wrong most often and strengthen those slides.
