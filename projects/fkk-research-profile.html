Only aggregate evidence is reproduced here

Historical snapshot, not a live university feed. Private working reports and individual records are not distributed.

Overlapping author profiles and inconsistent affiliations made the faculty’s publication records difficult to count.

I made the counting rules visible

I matched the staff roster to publication records, checked affiliations and removed duplicates. The Excel evidence base supports the interactive report.

The figures below show aggregate findings from that report.

From scattered records to one evidence base

Follow the evidence / pin a stage

01 / 114 academics

Start with the faculty

I used the supplied staff list, joined split author profiles and checked institutional identities.

02 / 1,637 credited papers · 2006–2026

Count a paper once

Each paper needed a qualifying faculty affiliation. I removed duplicates by publication identifier (DOI) and title, then counted each paper once regardless of co-authors.

03 / 950 papers · 2020–2026

Fix the reporting window

I used the same seven publication years throughout. The incomplete 2026 result is marked separately.

04 / 671 with quartiles · 279 without

Keep the missing category

I kept papers without a journal ranking visible. They are neither low-ranked journals nor silently excluded records.

The publication plate

The strongest complete year is 2025

Each mark is one credited paper. The 179 papers in 2025 exceed every other complete year shown; 2026 is partial.

Which papers have a journal ranking?

Q1 and Q2 are the top two of four journal ranking bands (quartiles). They rank journals, not individual papers; 29.4% of recent papers have no quartile.

A second index is a coverage check

I matched Web of Science (WoS) records to Scopus by DOI or title. Of 886 WoS records with an FKK academic, 783 match the faculty set; the remaining categories are not a complete two-database comparison.

Connect the output to a recognisable theme

I used Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) query tags to group research by themes such as energy and water. Tags indicate subject coverage, not demonstrated social outcomes.

The rules behind the report

Definition before display

Population

Faculty roster → matched author profiles → qualifying affiliation on the paper. Counts here use the credited-paper extract, not all publications on an author's profile.

One paper, one credit

Credit goes to the first author, otherwise the corresponding author, otherwise the only faculty author or first listed faculty author. DOI and title checks remove duplicates.

Separate questions

The affiliation-based peer benchmark has a different inclusion rule: 701 papers in 2022–2026 versus 752 in the credited-paper extract. I keep that benchmark out of these figures.

What this misses

  • Database coverage and author matching can omit or misattribute papers.
  • Journal quartiles do not measure a paper's originality or practical value.
  • SDG query tags can overlap and miss relevant work.
  • 2026 is partial. No year-end forecast is presented.
  • The credited-paper set and affiliation benchmark use different rules (752 versus 701 papers for 2022–2026). No time saving or faculty performance improvement is measured.

What I'd do next

Review unmatched records with the faculty, record each correction and refresh the roster and paper extract on a fixed date.
