Malaysian household income after inflation, by state

Method notes · Reproducible query walkthrough.

I joined household income to each state’s consumer price index (CPI), a measure of price changes. This gives inflation-adjusted income at the same survey dates.

The analysis describes household income, not individual salaries. Explore the national trend, then compare states.

One country. Different gains.

Terengganu leads over 2016–2024: +25.4%. Over 2019–2024, Selangor leads: +16.9%. A ranking is inseparable from its period.

04 / The state story

A higher income can still buy less.

In 2019–2024, Putrajaya’s nominal median rose 7.9%, but its real median fell 7.5%. Kuala Lumpur’s real median fell 5.2%. Perlis was down 0.2%.

05 / The state story

Read the colour. Keep the caveat.

State CPI tracks price change within a state; it does not compare price levels between states. These are descriptive ranks, with no sampling uncertainty supplied. Small differences are not significance tests.

Your state, your question.

The open data desk

Choose a measure, then click, tap or focus a state on the map, or use the state selector. Both show the same verified values.

The average is not the middle.

Median income is the middle household’s income when incomes are ordered; mean income is the average. High incomes pull the mean up, so it can overstate what a typical household earns.

What is left each month?

Mean gross income minus mean consumption spending is an aggregate gap, not household savings.

From the analyst’s notebook

Match the state. Match the year.

I averaged only complete CPI years, joined on state and year, and ranked before rounding. Missing values stay missing.

01_real_income_growth.sql · excerpt

SELECT i.state, CAST(substr(i.date,1,4) AS INTEGER) AS year,
           i.income_median, c.cpi, i.income_median * 100.0 / c.cpi AS real_income
    FROM income_state AS i
    -- JOIN matches records across tables; ON lists ALL matching keys.
    -- A state-only join would multiply each income row by many CPI years.
    JOIN annual_cpi AS c ON i.state = c.state AND substr(i.date,1,4) = CAST(c.year AS TEXT)
    WHERE c.year IN (2016, 2019, 2024)

Poverty tells another part of the story.

Sarawak’s reported absolute poverty fell from 10.8% to 8.4% between the 2022 and 2024 surveys: a 2.4 percentage-point decline, the largest in this comparison.

Prices do not rise together.

Food & Beverages rose 16.5% nationally from 2019 to 2024, compared with 6.7% for transport and 6.4% for housing, utilities and fuels. A household’s basket may feel very different from the average.

What this misses

  • Survey years only. No invented annual incomes; the national 2020 estimate is separately marked.
  • Gross household income is not individual pay, disposable income or adjusted for household size.
  • Mean and median answer different questions. Mean income less mean spending is not savings.
  • CPI base is 2010 = 100. State CPI measures within-state change, not relative price levels between states.
  • National geography and population coverage change. The long view is descriptive, not a like-for-like growth estimate.
  • Sampling uncertainty is unavailable. Rankings do not prove significance or causation.
  • Geographic area is not household count; the two map panels are fitted separately. Labuan and Putrajaya have shorter separate histories.

What I’d do next
