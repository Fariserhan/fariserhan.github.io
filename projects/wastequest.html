05 / Client work

A bilingual learning game built from a UPM teaching module

I turned a 22-slide teaching module on waste-to-wealth into WasteQuest: a free website where learners clean up a pixel town by doing labs, games and quizzes.

At a glance

Client
A UPM course team, Faculty of Engineering (Department of Chemical and Environmental Engineering)
What I did
  • Designed and built the whole website from the module.
  • Organised the content into 15 labs, 6 games, 5 simulations and a 120-question quiz bank.
  • Made every screen work in English and Bahasa Melayu, for four audience levels.
What they got
A free public website that runs on phones, tablets and classroom projectors, with no app or login.
Tools
HTML, CSS, JavaScript, structured content files
My role
Freelance research and data work · design and build

By Faris Erhan · Portfolio revised 17 October 2026

The starting point was a 22-slide PDF teaching module from UPM's Faculty of Engineering, written by six authors. It explained waste-to-wealth: turning rubbish into useful products.

A PDF is hard to use with a class. I rebuilt it as a game: the home screen is a small pixel-art town, and each building (Academy, Recycling Plant, Compost Farm, Maker Lab, Market, Arena) opens one part of the course. Earning badges cleans the town up.

See it live: wastequest.github.io ↗

Turning one document into structured content

Most of the work was organising material, the same job as preparing data. Labs, questions and badges are kept as consistent lists, so the site can filter labs, draw quizzes from one bank and show progress.

Hands-on labs, each with a video

There are 15 hands-on labs, such as candles from used cooking oil, pet treats from fish waste and a cabbage pH indicator. Ten came from 2025 student posters and five from the 2024 Zero-Plastic Hero student projects. Each idea was checked, made safe and adapted.

Learners can filter labs by age, time and whether heat is needed. Each lab shows safety and adult-supervision notes, and has its own short narrated video in English and in Malay, played from the site itself.

Games, simulations and a quiz bank

There are 6 games (sorting, matching, crossword, word search, myth-or-fact and a timed quiz) and 5 simulations, including pH, compost, eco-enzyme, a waste-to-cash business and a footprint calculator.

Eco Quiz Blast draws from a bank of 120 questions with a 20-second clock. Home missions add real tasks, such as a week of sorting household waste.

Built for real classrooms

The site runs on phones, tablets and classroom projectors. There are four audience levels: kids aged 7 to 12, teens aged 13 to 17, adults, and a teacher view with teaching notes.

Class Battle and Event Town

In Class Battle, a teacher hosts a live quiz on the projector and students join by phone with a 5-character code or a QR code. Team mode runs 2 to 4 teams on one screen with no phones or internet.

Event Town is for school events and booths: everyone's answers clean one shared town on the big screen.

Teachers, certificates and offline use

The Teacher Hub holds session plans, learning outcomes, safety rules, rubrics, curriculum links and credits. Three printable certificate tracks (Junior Eco-Hero, Zero-Waste Champion and Waste-to-Wealth Practitioner) are each unlocked by a final quiz plus badges. They are non-credit, designed around 20 hours.

For schools with poor internet there are printable booklets in English and Malay, and an offline pack: the site plus videos on a USB stick.

Privacy by design

Progress and badges are saved only on the learner's own device. Usage is counted without names. No account is needed.

What this misses

  • No learning-outcome or usage figures are reported here.
  • The flip-page eBook of the whole course (English and Malay, reading-level picker, PDF download) is on a preview version, not yet on the main site.
  • Screenshots were taken from the public site; the live site may change.

What I'd do next

Use the anonymous usage counts to see which labs and quiz questions are used most and where learners stop, then improve those parts first.
