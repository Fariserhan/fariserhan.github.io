05 / Freelance research and data work / UPM SPERC research database

3,312 publication rows are not 3,312 papers.

A research centre needed a reliable view of its work. I connected a master workbook, a performance dashboard and a strategic evidence pack, making the counting rules visible before turning records into conclusions.

A source snapshot, not a live performance report. Workbook rows include repeated contributions and historical records. Strategy figures use a separately defined set of unique papers. No individual performance records are published here.

Research arrives in pieces: publication lists, grant records, CV extracts and previous reports. A centre can have plenty of information and still struggle to answer a simple question: how much work does this number actually represent?

In this freelance research and data work, I brought those sources into a structured workbook and built views for different decisions. The dashboard made the evidence easier to explore; the strategic-plan work connected it to questions about research focus and future priorities.

The first deliverable was a definition

A publication row records a contribution in an extracted source. A paper can appear against more than one member. Before reporting centre output, the workflow filters the current roster, fixes the publication window and counts unique Scopus document identifiers.

One ledger, several decisions

Scroll / inspect / pin

01 / Collect

Keep the source visible

I consolidated CV extracts and research records into named workbook sheets. Source and inclusion fields preserve where a row came from and whether it belongs in an analysis.

02 / Define

Decide what counts

The strategy build selects current members, included Scopus records and publication years 2020–2026. It groups records by Scopus EID before calculating centre output. The workbook roster contains 20 current members.

03 / Explore

Make the evidence usable

I built a standalone HTML dashboard to explore output, research groups and data coverage. The available dashboard still labels a 19-member scenario, so its totals are not used for the numerical claims on this page.

04 / Explain

Carry the caveats forward

The strategy evidence pack summarises publication trends and overlapping research themes. I used that evidence to support the strategic-plan narrative, keeping observed results separate from proposals and future intentions.

Count the rows. Then explain the unit.

The workbook contains different kinds of activity. These are nonempty data rows, with the header excluded, across all membership and inclusion flags. They describe the material I had to organise; they do not measure unique centre outputs.

“A count needs a unit before it becomes evidence.”

My analysis of the source reconciliation

A reporting window changes the answer

The strategy snapshot contains 565 unique Scopus document identifiers for current members in 2020–2026. Its seven yearly counts sum to that total. Comparing the incomplete 2026 bar with a full calendar year would overstate a fall in output.

Scope check

565 in the strategy snapshot. 564 in the website extract.

The difference sits in 2022: 69 versus 68. I verified both files and kept their separate bases rather than silently replacing either result. The source of that one-document difference still needs tracing.

Reconcile the 2022 document sets before the next combined client report.

The average hides differences between themes

For strategic planning, total output is only a starting point. I compared selected themes using field-weighted citation impact (FWCI): 1.0 is the normalised reference, with higher values indicating more citations than expected for comparable research.

Biochar routes stand above that reference in this snapshot. Biohydrogen also does, but from only 14 papers. These are descriptive signals for further investigation, not a funding recommendation.

What this misses

  • CV-derived rows reflect available records, not a complete audit of every activity.
  • Theme matching uses titles and keywords. Categories overlap and may miss relevant work.
  • The available dashboard uses an older roster; its figures cannot be substituted for the strategy snapshot.
  • No measured time saving or subsequent research impact is claimed.

What I'd do next

Trace the 2022 discrepancy at document level, rebuild the dashboard against the agreed roster and attach one dated data manifest to every output.

Add completeness checks for identifiers, dates and metric coverage before refreshing the strategic evidence.

What the client could use

The deliverable connected a master research database, an explorable performance dashboard and strategic-plan evidence. Its value was a clearer route from an underlying record to a management question, with the counting rules available for review.

For a data analyst role, this is the core skill: organising messy evidence, choosing the right unit and explaining what a result can support.
